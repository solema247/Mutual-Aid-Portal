# Volunteer & Frontline Responder Support Analysis

## 1. Overview

**Focus:** Spending and support for volunteers and frontline responders who deliver ERR projects. This excludes protection activities aimed at the general community (evacuation of civilians, community food baskets, shelter for displaced families).

**Data snapshot:** August 2026 (Mutual Aid Portal). F1 Workplans, F4 Financial Reports, F5 Programmatic Reports.

**Summary data:** Emergency Rooms across Sudan rely on mutual aid volunteer networks to deliver humanitarian aid. This analysis tracks how funding supports those volunteers and responders across the F1, F4, and F5 pipeline: what was planned, what was spent, and what was reported.

**Research questions:**

How much support goes to the volunteers and frontline responders delivering ERR projects, in what forms, and with what reported impact?

What do the narratives behind the numbers describe?

**Summary data:** Volunteer protection accounts for $86,839 (8.4% of volunteer spend, 0.54% overall spend). Medical support is the largest share ($52,479), followed by evacuation and relocation ($30,711), with smaller amounts for a detention fine ($2,647) and psychosocial support ($350). Spending is concentrated in Khartoum and North Darfur. The remaining volunteer spend goes to communications ($23,596), incentives and stipends ($387,301), and other support ($530,793). Communications covers phones, internet, Starlink, and connectivity allowances. Other support includes equipment, transport, training, and general volunteer costs not classified elsewhere.

## 2. F1 Work Plans

F1 work plans describe the activities, budgets, and support that mutual aid groups intend to provide. The summary below is drawn from those submissions, identifying volunteer-related planning through activity categories and a review of objectives, budget descriptions, and stated needs.

Volunteer support is a common planned category in F1: 1,408 planned activities are tagged "Volunteer support", second only to logistics operations. Of 2,040 projects in the database, 1,382 include at least one planned activity in the Volunteer support category, and 1,420 include a volunteer-support budget line. Volunteers are also mentioned in project objectives for 155 projects, and in additional support fields for 526 projects, often describing needs such as training, equipment, or coordination support that extend beyond the budget lines.

**Budgets**

Groups planned $632,665 in volunteer support across 1,420 projects, an average of roughly $446 per project that budgeted any amount. Planning is split between general volunteer support and incentives or stipends, consistent with what was later reported in F4.

The most common budget labels are Volunteer support ($233,389 across 570 projects) and Volunteer incentives ($156,921 across 452 projects). Smaller amounts appear under combined medical and support lines ($16,340 across 7 projects) and volunteer wallets ($3,477 across 14 projects). Many other one-off labels account for the remainder.

Work plans also describe volunteer-related needs that are not fully costed in the budget, including capacity building, PPE, technical support, and guidance on record-keeping. Recurring themes include training volunteers and staff on humanitarian management and reporting (Central Darfur), PPE, tools, and transportation for workers and volunteers (South Darfur), and technical support for data collection and volunteer coordination (North Darfur).

## 3. F4 Financial Reports

F4 financial reports record what groups actually spent.

**Protection breakdown**

Of the $86,839 in volunteer protection spending across 46 projects, medical and injury support is the largest share ($52,479), followed by evacuation and relocation ($30,711). Smaller amounts cover a detention fine ($2,647), mental health and psychosocial support ($350), and danger and emergency costs ($652). Protection spending is concentrated in Khartoum (mainly medical support for volunteers) and North Darfur (evacuation, injury treatment, and the Volunteer Support Fund).

**Planned vs actual**

Of the 1,420 projects that planned volunteer support in F1, 952 also show matching volunteer spend in F4 ($565,756 actual against $632,665 planned, or 89.4% on matched projects). 645 projects planned volunteer support but show no matching F4 spend. This may reflect reporting lag, spending recorded under different activity labels, or plans that were not implemented.

Financial summary narratives mention volunteers or capacity building in 239 reports; 19 mention volunteers in lessons learned.

**Geographic spread**

Volunteer spending appears across many states. The highest totals are in Khartoum, North Darfur, West Kordofan, South Darfur, Kassala, and Sennar. Protection spending, by contrast, is much more concentrated, with a small number of projects accounting for most of the evacuation, medical, and security-related costs.

## 4. F5 Programmatic Reports

F5 programmatic reports describe what was delivered and what groups learned. Structured reach data explicitly tagged as volunteer support is limited: eight records across five projects, reporting 193 individuals reached.

Examples include food baskets for volunteers' families in Khartoum (160 individuals), volunteer medical treatment in Khartoum and East Darfur, volunteer support in Kassala, and the Volunteer Support Fund in North Darfur.

The narrative sections of F5 reports contain substantially more volunteer-related content than these structured reach rows alone. Recurring themes include:

The Volunteer Support Fund in North Darfur: evacuation from high-risk areas, medical treatment for volunteers' relatives, and training incentives, alongside reports of security delays and insufficient funding to support all volunteers in need.

Operational constraints on volunteers: delivery delays, security checkpoints, monitoring of volunteer movements, and currency exchange costs, particularly in West Darfur and Al Jazirah.

Medical support for volunteers: recurring Khartoum State Emergency Room payments described as covering volunteers' medical needs.

Communications and coordination: technical support, smartphones, and internet for coordinators in Khartoum and East Darfur.

Incentives tied to continued work amid blockades and insecurity.

## 5. Protection examples

**Medical and injury**

In Khartoum, the State Emergency Room reported repeated payments to cover volunteers' medical needs, including $5,979 for 18 volunteers and $3,397 for medical emergencies. Individual cases include gallbladder removal ($429), breast tumor removal ($309), a cesarean section ($429), and assistance with treatment for a volunteer's father ($429). Another Khartoum project paid $441 for chemotherapy sessions for a volunteer mother.

In North Darfur, the Volunteer Support Fund recorded $1,250 for treatment and surgery for a volunteer, $353 for a Country Kitchen volunteer injured by shrapnel, $526 for medical expenses for a volunteer's father, and $294 in support for a volunteer's family.

In South Kordofan, $200 was spent on psychological support for volunteers. In Sennar, $150 was spent on support sessions and psychological areas for volunteers.

**Evacuation and relocation**

In South Kordofan, $5,000 was spent on evacuating volunteers' families from Kadugli to safe areas. In Khartoum, $1,480 covered evacuation and protection of five families of volunteers from UmBada.

In North Darfur, recorded evacuation cases include $1,471 to treat and evacuate a female volunteer shot in Hasaknitah to Al-Daein; $1,053 to evacuate eight volunteers from the Abuliha emergency room after they were trapped in the Marqira forest; $882 to evacuate a volunteer's family of four from Dar es Salaam to Omdurman; $588 to evacuate volunteers from Tawila to Al-Tina and onward to Chad; $658 for evacuation costs for a volunteer's family; $500 to evacuate a volunteer to Omdurman; and $588 to evacuate a volunteer's mother from Tawila to Omdurman.

**Security threats and emergency**

In North Darfur, $2,647 was paid in fines imposed by the Rapid Support Forces to secure the release of six detained volunteers. In South Kordofan, $358 was spent to respond to emergency needs of volunteers.

## 6. Communications examples

Volunteer communications spending totals $23,596. Spending is concentrated in Khartoum and North Darfur, covering phones, internet access, laptops, and communication allowances for coordinators and volunteer networks.

**Phones and devices**

In Khartoum, $2,613 was spent on smartphones for 10 volunteers. Other Khartoum payments include individual smartphone purchases ($450 and $610) and laptops for volunteer technical support ($450 each).

**Internet and connectivity**

In Khartoum, $2,055 covered communication and internet for 15 volunteers, $700 covered communication and internet incentives for seven volunteers, $588 covered internet access for six volunteers, and $500 covered communications and internet for 10 volunteers.

In North Darfur, $559 covered internet expenses for volunteers attending a workshop.

In East Darfur, $186 covered food and internet costs for volunteers.

**Communication allowances**

In Khartoum, $700 covered communication and transportation allowances for volunteers, and $500 covered communication and transportation expenses.

In North Darfur, $319 covered incentives and communications for 10 volunteers, and $300 covered incentives and communications for 10 volunteer supervisors.

In South Kordofan, $353 covered communication expenses for volunteers. In Kassala, $200 covered volunteer communication expenses. In Sennar, $106 covered communication and transportation for three volunteers.
